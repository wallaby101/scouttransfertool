Four sizes. If you are not sure, leave it on 3 × 3.

How to · New

Keep artwork out of the stitching

Where: ✎ editor → Sewing buffer

The stitching around a label takes a band just inside its edge. A sewing buffer keeps every bit of artwork out of that band.

  1. Click ✎ on the logo’s row to open the editor.
  2. Under Sewing buffer, pick 3 mm, 4 mm or ¼″. The logo shrinks to fit inside a magenta line that far in from the edge.
  3. With more than one logo, tick Use on every logo to set the whole job at once.
  4. Click Save. Every label on the sheet now shows its magenta line.
Good to know
  • The magenta line is a guide and never prints. Untick Show sewing outline to hide it.
  • Scale a logo up or drag it over the line and the overhang is cut off, not printed. A yellow note says by how much.
  • A buffer costs small labels the most. At ¼″, a 2.5 × 1.3″ label keeps under half its area for artwork.
  • The default is None, so older jobs print exactly as before.
  • Not the same as Cut margin: that is film outside the label for the scissors; this is the band inside it for the needle.
How to

Put a logo on a coloured label

Where: ✎ editor → Background → Color
  1. Open the logo’s editor with ✎.
  2. Next to Background, click Color.
  3. Click the colour swatch beside it and pick the label colour.
  4. Click Save. The colour fills the whole label behind the logo.

The logo itself is never recoloured

For a white logo on a navy label, ask the customer for a white logo file; the tool supplies the navy. With a sewing buffer set, the colour still runs to the label edge while the artwork stays inside the line.

How to · New

Remove a stray line or empty space

Where: ✎ editor → Image → Trim edges

Exported logos sometimes carry a one-pixel line along an edge, too thin to see on screen but printed on every label. A yellow note under the logo warns you: Thin line along the bottom edge.

  1. Open the logo’s editor with ✎.
  2. Click Trim edges. The tool finds the line or empty margin and draws a box around what to keep. Or click Crop… and drag the box yourself.
  3. Check the readout: the size the artwork prints and its DPI. Amber means under 270 DPI, red under 200.
  4. Click Apply crop, then Save.

The file is never changed, so Undo crop always puts the whole image back. Trimming empty space makes the logo print bigger, which lowers its DPI; the readout shows by how much.

How to

Print a small order

Where: Sheet Size, and the counter under your logos

You never pick a half sheet. Choose the full sheet you buy, set your quantities, and the tool uses a half sheet whenever the last sheet would be half empty.

  1. Under Sheet Size, pick the full sheet you buy: 22 × 24″ or 22 × 22″.
  2. Set each logo’s quantity.
  3. Read the counter under the logos: 45 labels — 1 full sheet + 1 half sheet.
  4. If your printer only takes full sheets, tick Always use full sheets. The tool remembers it.

How the tool decides

A job fills in jig halves. Every two of them is one full sheet, and a leftover one is a half sheet — the top half of the film, 12″ tall instead of 24. In the jig layout it is one jig half, pressed in the jig exactly as a full sheet is; in the packed layout it is packed on its own. It is always the last sheet.

Sixteen labels become a whole sheet rather than two halves, because two halves are the same film and twice the pressing. Forty-five become a full sheet and a half rather than two full sheets, because the second full sheet would be half empty.

  • Half sheets are 22 × 12″ under a 22 × 24″ sheet and 22 × 11″ under a 22 × 22″ one.
  • A PDF of a mixed job has pages of both sizes, each at 300 DPI.
  • With Always use full sheets ticked, 45 labels at 3 × 3″ become two full sheets instead of a full and a half.
How to

Fit more labels on a sheet

Where: Layout → Packed — cut by hand

If you cut the transfers out by hand rather than pressing them in the jig, packed fills the whole sheet with a ½″ border, and fits up to 40% more.

  1. Set Layout to Packed — cut by hand.
  2. Type a Cut margin in inches, 0 to 1: how far each dotted cut line sits from the label edge. 0.125 is the default.
  3. Read the note underneath, for example 36 per sheet — 6 x 6. Each piece cut out is 3.5 x 3.5″. It updates as you type.
  4. Keep the margin wider than the bleed. If it is not, the tool warns you.

2.5 × 1.3″ and 2 × 2″ have no jig yet

Packed works today, because packing needs no jig. Jig slots will not line up until a jig has been cut to match them, so use packed for those sizes for now.

What else changes in packed mode
  • Every piece you cut out is the label plus the cut margin all round. Neighbours share the cut line between them.
  • Bleed and the outline checkboxes work as they do for the jig. The green outline is the label; the dotted red line is where you cut. If a logo does not reach the green line on every side, that is the shape of the logo file, not the spacing.
  • No blue jig boxes and no cyan line down the middle. One blue outline shows the printed area.
  • A half sheet is packed on its own, so it is not simply half of a full one — and it may lay the logos the other way round. That makes no difference once they are cut out.
How to

Resize, turn or move a logo

Where: ✎ editor

Everything in the editor applies to that one logo, on every copy of it.

  • Make it bigger or smaller: drag Scale. 100% fills as much of the label as it can without cropping; past 100% crops the edges.
  • Turn it: drag Rotate, or use the quarter-turn buttons. The logo is re-fitted after turning, so nothing is cut off.
  • Move it: the six arrows push it to an edge and the dot re-centres it, or drag it on the picture.
  • Start over: Reset puts scale, turn and position back. The background, buffer and crop stay.
How to

Add names or numbers

Where: ✎ editor → + Add Text, then Variable Data
  1. In the editor, click + Add Text and set the font, size, colour and position. {{name}} in the preview is a placeholder and never prints.
  2. Click Save.
  3. In the Variable Data panel on the left, type one value per label, separated by commas, and click Apply.
  4. Or tick Same text on every label and type it once. Commas stay intact, so Smith, Jr. survives as one name.

Text will not print without values

Styling the text is not enough. If the Variable Data box is empty, no text is printed at all and nothing warns you on the sheet itself. Check the counter reads green before you download.

How to

Send the right file to the printer

  1. Pick your printer under Print Service. The tool checks the sheet against that company’s published limits.
  2. Wait for the chips to go green. A red one blocks the download and offers a one-click fix.
  3. Click the outlined download button — for Transfer Express, Download PNG.

Good to send

✓ Sheet 22 x 22″ ✓ Logo 600 DPI

Both green. Nothing between you and the download.

Stop

× Sheet 22 x 24″ ✓ Logo 600 DPI

The download greys out and a red box explains why, with a button that fixes it.

The expensive mistake

The sheet size trap

This one cost a real job. Transfer Express accept an uploaded file up to 22 × 22″. Send a 22 × 24″ sheet and they do not refuse it — they shrink the whole thing to fit.

Every label comes out undersized

A 3″ label prints at 2.75″, and the jig does not line up. Nothing on their page says they changed your file — it just shows the new size.

The tool catches it

The download greys out and a Switch to 22 × 22″ button changes the setting for you.

Why their bigger sheet does not help

They do sell a 24 × 22″ gang sheet, but only if you build it inside their own online designer. A file you upload is always treated as a single image, and their maximum single image is 22 × 22″.

Send the right file

PNG, not PDF

Transfer Express cannot read resolution inside a PDF

The identical sheet uploads as 300 DPI when you send the PNG, and draws a “less than 150 dots per inch” warning as a PDF. Both files are the same 300 DPI sheet.

Download PNG Download PDF

The app outlines the button to use in green. The PDF is still the easier file to look over on screen before committing film — just do not upload it.

Bigger is fine — the tool scales it down cleanly. Smaller is what causes soft edges. Below 150 DPI, Transfer Express’s printer adds a white jagged line around the artwork.

What Transfer Express expects, beyond what the tool can check
  • Lines thinner than 0.018″ print, but they cannot put adhesive there, so those parts may not last.
  • Soft edges that fade away to nothing will not print. Avoid drop shadows and feathered edges.
  • They print CMYK, which cannot reproduce fluorescent or metallic colours.
  • Set Remove White to Original. These sheets are already transparent, so the only white it could remove is white inside your logos.
Staying current

When a new version appears

A small dark bar appears at the bottom of the window saying A new version is ready. There is nothing to download and nothing to install. See what changed →

Nothing changes until you click Reload

If you are mid-job, finish it and download your sheets first. The version you are using carries on working exactly as it was. Your logos and settings are not kept across a reload.

There is no limit on how many labels a job can have — it adds sheets as it needs them. The one thing it insists on is a project name before anything will download.
