How to

Make a sheet in five steps

  1. 1
    Type a project name

    It’s printed small in the sheet margin and used in the file name. The download buttons stay grey until you enter one.

  2. 2
    Pick the logo size

    There are four. If you aren’t sure, leave it on 3 × 3.

  3. 3
    Drag your logo in

    Drop the file on the dashed box, or click the box to choose it. Use a PNG with a transparent background if you have one. A JPEG works, but it can’t be transparent, so its background prints as a white box.

  4. 4
    Set how many you need

    Use − and +, or click the number and type it. The counter underneath shows how many sheets that makes, for example 45 labels — 1 full sheet + 1 half sheet.

  5. 5
    Download

    When the printer panel is green, click the download button. For one sheet it says Download PNG. For more than one it says Save PNGs to Folder and asks where to put them. Send those files.

How to · New

Keep artwork out of the stitching

Where: ✎ editor → Sewing buffer

When a label is sewn on, the stitching runs through a band just inside its edge. A sewing buffer keeps the artwork out of that band.

  1. 1
    Open the editor

    Click ✎ on the logo’s row.

  2. 2
    Pick a buffer

    Under Sewing buffer, pick 3 mm, 4 mm or ¼″. A magenta line appears that far in from the edge, and the logo shrinks to fit inside it.

  3. 3
    Use it on every logo, if you want

    If the job has more than one logo, tick Use on every logo to give them all the same buffer.

  4. 4
    Save

    Click Save. Each label in the sheet preview now shows its magenta line.

More about the sewing buffer
  • The magenta line is only on screen and is never printed. Untick Show sewing outline to hide it.
  • If you scale the logo up or drag it across the line, the part outside is cut off. A yellow note says how far it crosses.
  • A buffer takes a bigger share of a small label. With a ¼″ buffer, a 2.5 × 1.3″ label has less than half its area left for artwork.
  • New logos start at None, so jobs made before the buffer existed print the same as they always did.
  • Cut margin, in the packed layout, is a different setting. It is film left around the outside of the label for cutting, whereas the sewing buffer is a band inside the label.
How to

Put a logo on a coloured label

Where: ✎ editor → Background → Color
  1. Open the logo’s editor with ✎.
  2. Next to Background, click Color.
  3. Click the colour swatch beside it and pick the label colour.
  4. Click Save. The colour fills the label behind the logo.

The logo keeps its own colours

The tool can’t recolour a logo. For a white logo on a navy label, get a white version of the logo from the customer and set the background to navy. If a sewing buffer is set, the colour still runs to the label edge and only the artwork moves in.

How to · New

Remove a stray line or empty space

Where: ✎ editor → Image → Trim edges

Logo exports sometimes have a 1-pixel line along one edge. It’s too thin to notice on screen, but it prints on every label. When the tool finds one, it puts a yellow note under the logo, such as Thin line along the bottom edge.

  1. 1
    Open the editor

    Click ✎ on the row with the yellow note.

  2. 2
    Click Trim edges

    The tool finds the line or the empty margin and puts a box around the part to keep. To place the box yourself, click Crop… and drag it.

  3. 3
    Check the readout

    It gives the printed size and the DPI. Amber means under 270 DPI, and red means under 200.

  4. 4
    Apply and save

    Click Apply crop, then Save. The yellow note goes away.

Your file isn’t changed, so Undo crop always brings the whole image back. Trimming empty space makes the logo print larger, which lowers its DPI. The readout shows the new figure.

How to

Print a small order

Where: Sheet Size, and the counter under your logos

You only choose the full sheet size. If the last sheet of a job would be half full or less, the tool prints it as a half sheet.

  1. Under Sheet Size, pick the full sheet you buy: 22 × 24″ or 22 × 22″.
  2. Set each logo’s quantity.
  3. The counter under the logos shows the result, for example 45 labels — 1 full sheet + 1 half sheet.
  4. If your printer only takes full sheets, tick Always use full sheets. The tool remembers this.

How the tool decides

Labels fill one jig half at a time, and two halves make a full sheet. If one half is left over, it becomes a half sheet: the top half of the film, 12″ tall. It is always the last sheet of the job. In the jig layout you press it in the jig like a full sheet. In the packed layout it is packed separately.

Two half sheets use the same film as one full sheet and take twice the pressing, so 16 labels go on one full sheet. At 45, a second full sheet would be half empty, so the last 15 go on a half sheet.

  • A half sheet is 22 × 12″ when you buy 22 × 24″ sheets, and 22 × 11″ when you buy 22 × 22″.
  • The PDF of a mixed job has pages of both sizes, all at 300 DPI.
  • With Always use full sheets ticked, 45 labels at 3 × 3″ make two full sheets.
How to

Fit more labels on a sheet

Where: Layout → Packed (cut by hand)

If you cut the transfers out by hand instead of pressing them through the jig, the packed layout uses the whole sheet inside a ½″ border and fits more labels.

  1. Set Layout to Packed — cut by hand.
  2. Type a Cut margin in inches, from 0 to 1. It is the distance from the label edge to the dotted cut line. The default is 0.125.
  3. The note underneath updates as you type, for example 36 per sheet — 6 x 6. Each piece cut out is 3.5 x 3.5″.
  4. Make the margin larger than the bleed. The tool warns you if it isn’t.

2.5 × 1.3″ and 2 × 2″ have no jig yet

Packed works for these sizes now, since it doesn’t use a jig. Jig slots won’t line up until a jig is cut to match, so use packed for them.

Other differences in packed
  • Each piece you cut out is the label plus the cut margin on every side. Neighbouring labels share a cut line.
  • Bleed and the outline checkboxes behave as they do in the jig layout. The green outline is the label edge and the red dotted line is where you cut. If a logo stops short of the green line on one side, the logo file is a different shape from the label.
  • There are no blue jig boxes and no cyan line across the middle. One blue outline marks the printed area.
  • A half sheet is packed separately, so it may not hold exactly half as many labels, and its logos may be turned the other way. The finished labels are the same either way, because each piece is cut out before it’s pressed.
How to

Resize, turn or move a logo

Where: ✎ editor

Changes in the editor apply to that logo and every copy of it.

  • Drag Scale to resize the logo. At 100% it fills as much of the label as it can without being cropped. Above 100%, the edges are cropped.
  • Drag Rotate, or use the quarter-turn buttons, to turn it. The logo is refitted after turning so its corners stay inside the label.
  • Use the six arrow buttons to push the logo to an edge, or the dot to centre it. You can also drag it on the picture.
  • Reset puts the scale, rotation and position back to the start. It leaves the background, sewing buffer and crop as they are.
How to

Add names or numbers

Where: ✎ editor → + Add Text, then Variable Data
  1. In the editor, click + Add Text and set the font, size, colour and position. The {{name}} in the preview is a placeholder and isn’t printed.
  2. Click Save.
  3. In the Variable Data panel on the left, type one value per label with commas between them, and click Apply.
  4. If every label should say the same thing, tick Same text on every label and type it once. Commas are kept, so Smith, Jr. stays one name.

Text will not print without values

If the Variable Data box is empty, the labels print with only the logo, and the sheet gives no warning. Make sure the counter is green before you download.

How to

Send the right file to the printer

  1. Choose your printer under Print Service. The tool checks the sheet against the limits that company publishes.
  2. Wait for the chips to turn green. A red chip blocks the download and shows a button that fixes the problem.
  3. Click the download button with the outline. For Transfer Express that is Download PNG.

Can’t send yet

The download button is greyed out. A red box explains the problem and has a button to fix it.

Sheet size

Transfer Express shrink a 22 × 24″ sheet

Transfer Express accept uploads up to 22 × 22″. If you send a 22 × 24″ sheet, they shrink it to fit instead of rejecting it.

Every label prints too small

A 3″ label comes out at 2.75″ and no longer lines up with the jig. Their site shows the new size, but it doesn’t say the file was changed. This has already happened on one job.

The tool blocks the download

It greys out the download and shows a Switch to 22 × 22″ button that changes the setting.

Why their larger sheet doesn’t help

Transfer Express do sell a 24 × 22″ gang sheet, but only for sheets built in their own online designer. An uploaded file counts as a single image, and the largest single image they take is 22 × 22″.

File type

Send Transfer Express the PNG

Transfer Express can’t read the resolution of a PDF

The PNG uploads as 300 DPI. The PDF of the same sheet is also 300 DPI, but their uploader can’t read its resolution and warns that it is under 150 dots per inch.

Download PNG Download PDF

The app outlines the button to use in green. The PDF is easier to look over on screen before you print, but don’t upload it to Transfer Express.

Larger files are fine, since the tool scales them down cleanly. Smaller files print with soft edges, and below 150 DPI Transfer Express’s printer adds a jagged white line around the artwork.

Transfer Express requirements the tool can’t check
  • Lines thinner than 0.018″ print, but there’s no adhesive under them, so they may not last.
  • Edges that fade out, such as drop shadows and feathering, don’t print.
  • They print in CMYK, which can’t produce fluorescent or metallic colours.
  • Set Remove White to Original. The sheets are already transparent, so any white it removed would come out of your logos.
Updates

When a new version appears

A dark bar appears at the bottom of the window saying A new version is ready. You don’t need to download or install anything. See what changed →

Nothing changes until you click Reload

The version you have open keeps working until then. Reloading clears your logos and settings, so finish and download the job you’re on first.

There’s no limit on how many labels a job can have, and the tool adds sheets as it needs them. You do need a project name before anything will download.
